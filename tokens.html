Part 1 - Tokens: How AI Reads

Before an AI can do anything with your words, it has to convert them into something a computer can process. That conversion is called tokenisation, and it shapes everything about how AI works.

Computers don't read words

A computer, at its heart, only works with numbers. So the very first step in any language model is turning your text into numbers. But how do you chop up a sentence?

You might guess "one number per word", but that has problems. English alone has hundreds of thousands of words, plus names, typos, slang, and new words invented daily. You'd need an impossibly large dictionary, and any word not in it would break the system.

You could go the other way - one number per letter. That works, but it makes every sentence extremely long, and it throws away useful structure ("ing" and "tion" are meaningful chunks).

The compromise is tokens: chunks that are usually parts of words, whole short words, or common word pieces. Common words like "the" get their own token. Rarer words get split: "tokenisation" might become tokenisation or tokenisation.

Imagine LEGO. You could build a house out of individual studs (letters - too fiddly) or buy a pre-made house (whole words - too inflexible, you'd need a warehouse of every possible house). Instead, LEGO gives you a manageable set of bricks in common shapes that combine to build anything. Tokens are the LEGO bricks of language.

Notice a few things when you experiment:

Short, common words tend to survive as single tokens. Long or unusual words get split into pieces. Punctuation often becomes its own token. And spaces usually get attached to the start of the following token - that's why you'll sometimes see tokens like " fox" with a leading space.

Every token becomes a number

Each token in the model's vocabulary has an ID number. "The" might be token 464, " fox" might be token 21831. Your sentence becomes a list of numbers, and that is what the model actually processes. When the model responds, it produces numbers too, which get converted back into text for you to read.

Why should you care about tokens?

Tokens explain several everyday quirks of AI tools:

Pricing and limits. AI services measure usage in tokens, not words. A rough rule of thumb for English: 1 token ≈ ¾ of a word, so 1,000 tokens is roughly 750 words.

The context window. Every model has a maximum number of tokens it can consider at once - its "working memory". More on this in Part 3.

Odd weaknesses. Ever seen an AI struggle to count the letters in "strawberry"? That's tokens. The model may see strawberry as two chunks - it doesn't naturally see individual letters at all.

Key takeaway AI doesn't read words or letters - it reads tokens, which are numeric chunks of text. Many of AI's strengths and blind spots trace straight back to this.
